# Graduating from paper to real money

PaperTrench exists so you can find out whether you can trade this market
*before* the market charges you tuition. This page is the honest version of
"when am I ready?" — no hype, no gatekeeping, just the arithmetic.

*(Educational guidance about practice methodology — not financial advice. Real
trading risks real losses regardless of paper results.)*

## The one-way rule

Paper results transfer asymmetrically:

- **Paper failure is definitive.** If you cannot make fake money grow with
  zero pressure, real money — with fees, slippage, latency, and fear — will go
  worse. There is no known exception to this.
- **Paper success is necessary, not sufficient.** Real fills are worse than
  simulated ones, and real losses hurt in a way that changes your behavior.
  Passing the bar below means you've earned a *small, careful* start — not a
  bankroll.

## The bar

Work through these in order. The dashboard gives you every number.

1. **Sample size: 50+ closed round trips.** Ten trades prove nothing — a
   coin-flip strategy looks brilliant over ten trades about 5% of the time,
   and 5% of everyone is a lot of people posting screenshots.
2. **Positive expectancy over your last 30 rounds.** Average P&L per round,
   *after* the simulated fees, above zero — and not carried entirely by one
   outlier. Cover the best round with your hand: still positive?
3. **Your losers are smaller than your winners.** Average loss < average win.
   If your win rate is high but your average loss is 3× your average win,
   you have the classic blow-up profile: many small wins, one account-ending
   hold.
4. **Hold-time symmetry.** If you hold losers 5× longer than winners, you cut
   flowers and water weeds. The round table shows held time per trade —
   compare the two averages.
5. **No revenge pattern.** Look at your journal after each red round: did you
   re-enter the same coin within minutes, bigger? That pattern converts small
   losses into large ones, and it survives the transition to real money
   perfectly.
6. **A written thesis on most entries.** If the thesis box is usually empty,
   you weren't trading a plan — you were clicking. The habit is the point;
   nobody grades the prose.
7. **You survived a cold streak without changing size.** At least one 5-loss
   stretch in the journal where the position size stayed flat. Doubling after
   losses is how accounts die.

## If you pass

- Start at a fraction of what you can afford to lose — the first weeks of
  real trading are for calibrating *execution* (fills, slippage, speed), not
  for making money. Expect your real numbers to be worse than paper by at
  least the spread and your own hesitation.
- Keep journaling. The habit that got you here is the habit that keeps you
  here.
- If real results diverge hard from paper over a similar sample, come back.
  The simulator is free; re-tuition isn't.

## If you don't pass

That is the product working. You just learned — for free — what most people
in the trenches pay four or five figures to learn. Fix the specific line that
failed (the dashboard shows you which), and run another 50.
