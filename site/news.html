The big ones

Features worth a proper read

The releases that changed what PaperTrench is — each one with the story, the mechanism, and the honest limits.

v2.6.0 Feature Aug 5, 2026

X-Ray: every coin an account has ever posted, before you click buy

Open any X profile — or any post — and the intel is already on screen. Every contract address that account has put out, dated by the post itself. Bio, name and @handle changes, counted separately. The biggest accounts following them. No button, no "analyze", no waiting — and every counter says exactly how long we have been watching, because nobody can honestly tell you a bio changed on a day they never saw it.

v2.4.0 Speed Aug 5, 2026

Instant X links: 3.5 seconds down to about half a second

Vetting a coin means clicking its X link — and then watching a cold tab boot the entire X app. PaperTrench keeps one warm viewer tab and routes clicks into it as in-page navigations. Hovering starts the trip before you even click.

v2.7.0 Fix Aug 5, 2026

The "instant +14%" is dead: fills land on the chart you are looking at

On migrated tokens the price feed could silently lose one side of every trade, filling paper buys up to ~13% away from the live chart — and booking fake profit that teaches exactly the wrong lesson. Two independent fixes, one of them belt-and-braces.

v2.1.0 Feature Aug 5, 2026

The After: what actually happened once you sold

Every closed round watches its coin for the next hour and records the truth — observed extremes only, no interpolation. The most expensive guesswork in this market, and the number one revenge-FOMO trigger, replaced with measurement.

v2.5.0 Feature Aug 5, 2026

The Flex Pack: share cards that can never pass as a real trade

A terminal-grade composer with your own backgrounds, honest USD sub-lines, and an After line no other terminal can print. The PAPER watermark is drawn last, by a code path that reads no settings.

v2.2.0 Feature Aug 5, 2026

Fees & costs: make paper fills cost what real fills cost

Platform percentage, priority fee and bribe — the costs that dominate small entries and that zero-cost practice quietly ignores. A dust exit can now genuinely net negative, which is precisely the lesson.
